Fish feeding intensity assessment (FFIA) lets feeding machines respond to the appetite of the fish.
Audio and video complement each other here: the camera sees aggregation and surface agitation, while a
hydrophone hears feeding through darkness, reflections and turbidity. However, existing audio-visual
models are large, fuse the modalities in ad hoc ways at a single depth, and are evaluated under
incompatible protocols. We propose HyperAVNet, a parameter-efficient audio-visual network built from
hypercomplex layers, whose Hamilton-product weight sharing divides the parameters of every projection
by the algebra's dimension. Its central component, hypercomplex component fusion (HCF), encodes the
audio view, the video view and their multiplicative agreement as the components of a single quaternion
or octonion and mixes them with one Hamilton product. HCF blocks can be attached at any depth of the
two backbones, with zero-initialised cross-modal feedback, which enables a controlled study from late
to early fusion. A compact student is trained with logit-, embedding- and relation-level distillation
from pretrained audio and video teachers, ten of which we compare. On the AV-FFIA dataset, HyperAVNet
reaches \resOurAcc\% accuracy with \resOurParams\,M parameters and \resOurGMACs\,GMACs per clip,
$10\times$ fewer parameters and $13\times$ fewer operations than U-FFIA. \todo{One sentence placing this
